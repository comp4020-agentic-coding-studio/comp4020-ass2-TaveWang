# Week 9

## First aid, reporting, and wildlife rescue

### Safety First, Then Everything Else

---

## Safety, before anything is recorded

Is the vehicle off the traffic lane? Are hazard lights on? Is anyone hurt?

Nothing else in this lecture applies until that's settled.

```notes
Say this slowly. It's the one instruction in this week that must land
unambiguously — no jokes on this slide.
```

---

## Three real tasks — not four

1. ensure safety
2. contact the right service
3. give useful information

There is no fourth task. Treating or handling the animal isn't taught here,
and isn't something an untrained person should attempt.

---

## Observation, not guesswork

- location precise enough to find again
- number of animals, species if confident
- condition **observed**, not guessed

"It didn't move after I looked" — observation.
"It's probably fine" — guess wearing an observation's clothes.

---

## What this lecture does not teach

Do not approach for a better photo or a species ID.

Large macropods, snakes, monitor lizards, bats, koalas, birds of prey — the
categories most consistently flagged as untrained-handling risks.

This is a hard boundary, not a judgement call for the person at the scene.

---

## DRSABCD — named, not reproduced

Danger · Response · Send for help · Airway · Breathing · CPR ·
Defibrillation

St John Australia's own fact sheet says this "is not a substitute for
first aid training." A slide is a worse substitute than their fact sheet.

Human casualty involved → **000**, before anything else.

## Three kinds of guidance

Legal obligation. Organisational process. General advice.

Keep them visibly separate — don't blend "the law requires this" with
"WIRES asks for this" with "this is just sensible."
