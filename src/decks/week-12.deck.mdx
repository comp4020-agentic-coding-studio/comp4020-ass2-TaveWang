## Six moves a marker checks for

1. Problem
2. Evidence (≥3 strands)
3. Mechanism
4. Intervention (one primary measure)
5. Implementation (jurisdiction, approval, cost)
6. Evaluation (process metric vs outcome metric)

A gap in any one of these is a gap a marker will find.

## Same road, improved — Problem + Evidence

Kestrel Pass Road (fictional), 4.2km rural two-lane, NSW.

**Physics** (Week 4): higher speed → more collision energy — why speed
belongs in the discussion. Not a severity prediction: real injury also
depends on animal mass, impact geometry, vehicle structure.

**Ecology**: continuous scrub, no break — a clue worth investigating, not
proof this site has high crossing demand. Needs a movement survey.

**Engineering**: Rytwinski et al. (2016) — fencing+crossing = 83%
reduction; roadside reflectors ≈1%; crossings without fencing = no
detectable effect.

```notes
The old version cited "Week 1" for kinetic energy and "signage alone" for
the near-zero figure — both wrong. KE lives in Week 4. The near-zero
figure is reflectors, not signage.
```

## Improved — Mechanism + Intervention

**Mechanism**: fencing excludes animals from the carriageway; crossing
structure preserves the movement route. Full-length fencing *reduces* the
Week 7 end-of-fence problem — it doesn't eliminate it. Both endpoints
still need their own animal-movement/collision-risk check.

**Intervention**: exclusion fencing, full length, plus one crossing
structure. One primary measure. Midpoint is a **candidate** site pending
survey, terrain and engineering checks — not confirmed.

## Improved — Implementation + Evaluation

Illustrative only, reusing Week 7's fictional assumptions — **not** a real
NSW quote: **$756,000** initial, **$25,200** annual.

Approval authority depends on road classification (TfNSW vs council) —
**to be confirmed**. Vegetation/fencing work may trigger an environmental
or threatened-species check — **to be confirmed**.

**Process metric**: crossing-structure usage (camera trap).
**Outcome metric**: exposure-adjusted collision rate, pre/post, Week 3
method — not a raw count.
