## A prediction

If a vehicle's speed doubles, does its kinetic energy double?

Write your answer down. No calculating yet.

```notes

Most predictions say "yes, it doubles" — that's the instinct this lecture

```

---

## The equation

> E = ½ m v²

- *m* = mass in kilograms
- *v* = speed in **metres per second**
- *E* comes out in joules

> speed (m/s) = speed (km/h) ÷ 3.6

Mixing up km/h and m/s is the most common way to get this wrong.

---

## Worked calculation

Synthetic scenario, used all lecture: a **1,500 kg** vehicle.

| Speed | m/s | Kinetic energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40 km/h | 11.1 | **92.6 kJ** |

E = ½ × 1,500 × (40 / 3.6)² ≈ 92,600 J

All figures are illustrative teaching inputs, not measured values.

## Vehicle energy is not injury severity

- this is the **vehicle's** kinetic energy — mass and speed only
- never substitute an animal's mass into this same formula
- not a measure of energy transferred to an animal
- not a prediction of injury severity for either party

The calculator is not predicting an injury. It is explaining why speed
deserves a place in the discussion.

## Reaction distance

> reaction distance = speed × reaction time

Teaching assumption: **1.5 s** reaction time.

| Speed | Reaction distance |
| --- | --- |
| 40 km/h | 16.7 m |
| 60 km/h | 25.0 m |
| 80 km/h | 33.3 m |

Scales directly with speed — nothing squared yet.

---

## Braking distance

> braking distance = v² / (2a)

Teaching assumption: constant deceleration, **a = 6.5 m/s²**.

| Speed | Braking distance |
| --- | --- |
| 40 km/h | 9.5 m |
| 60 km/h | 21.4 m |
| 80 km/h | 38.0 m |

Same squared relationship as kinetic energy.

## Change one assumption

Wet-road deceleration: a = 5.0 m/s² instead of 6.5 m/s².

| Speed | Dry (6.5 m/s²) | Wet (5.0 m/s²) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40 km/h | 26.2 m | 29.0 m |
| 60 km/h | 46.4 m | 52.8 m |
| 80 km/h | 71.3 m | 82.7 m |

One changed assumption, every total shifts — check which assumption you're
relying on before trusting a number.

## What the model leaves out

- road surface
- visibility
- animal movement
- impact geometry
- vehicle deformation
- braking conditions

A simple model buys its clarity by leaving things out. This is the honest
list of what it left out here.
