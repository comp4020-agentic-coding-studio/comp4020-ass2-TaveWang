# Week 11

## Does the evidence travel?

### Moose, Deer, and the Limits of Borrowing

---

## Where the strongest evidence comes from

Moose in Scandinavia. Deer in North America.

Not kangaroos. Not Australia.

That's not a flaw in the literature — it's where the big, long-running
studies happened to be done.

---

## Five questions before importing a finding

1. Same animal?
2. Same road type?
3. Same mechanism?
4. What was actually measured?
5. What local-context differences matter?

```notes
None of "moose," "deer," or "kangaroo" is internally uniform either — walk
through the forest-moose vs tundra-moose example if time allows.
```

---

## The outcome-type ladder

1. Usage rate
2. Collision reduction
3. Exposure-adjusted rate
4. Injury outcome
5. Cost-effectiveness

High usage ≠ fewer collisions. Week 7's unfenced crossing structures proved
that already.

---

## Principle vs parameter

**Principles transfer.** Fencing excludes animals from the carriageway,
anywhere.

**Parameters don't.** The exact percentage, the exact predictor weights,
the exact cost per km — those are local facts about one study site.

Pilot effect size ≠ national rollout effect size, for the same reason.

## Testing it on Grellan Creek Road

22km, ~900 traffic/day, weekly patrol, mostly commuter-reported.

Same animal? No — kangaroo, not deer.
Same road type? Partly — rural two-lane, not an interstate.
Same mechanism? Plausible.
What local differences matter? Weekly, commuter-biased reporting stacks
the Week 3 exposure problem on top of the transfer question.

**Verdict**: mechanism is a reasonable hypothesis. The effect size is not
a promise.

## Next: the final project

At least three strands, and overseas evidence can be one of them —

but only the mechanism travels for free. Any specific number needs to be
