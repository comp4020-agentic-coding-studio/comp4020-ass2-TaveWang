## Law, liability and insurance after a strike

### "No One's At Fault" Doesn't Mean "No One Pays"

---

## Four different questions

1. who's at fault?
2. what does insurance cover?
3. who's contractually liable?
4. what does it actually cost?

Casual conversation collapses these into one. They're answered by four
different documents.

```notes
Ask the room which of the four they think they were actually asking when
they last said "so who's liable for that." Most will realise it was #2.
```

## Rental changes the document, not the physics

A rental agreement is a separate damage-waiver document, not your own
comprehensive policy wearing a costume.

Your personal policy often doesn't extend to a car you don't own at all.

---

## The actual-knowledge standard (NSW)

Civil Liability Act 2002 (NSW) s45: a roads authority isn't liable for not
doing road work unless it had **actual knowledge of the particular risk**.

General "kangaroos are around here" ≠ actual knowledge of **this stretch**.

```notes
Brodie v Singleton Shire Council [2001] HCA 29 is why this section exists —
it replaced a blanket immunity with something narrower but still real.
One state's statute. Say that out loud before generalising it.
```

## Three cases

**A** — own vehicle, direct strike → comprehensive, not-at-fault.

**B** — rental vehicle, swerved and crashed → rental damage-waiver terms,
not a personal policy.

**C** — "the authority must have known" → no record of *particular*
knowledge of this stretch. Illustrative reasoning, not legal advice.

## Claims data ≠ true incidence

An insurer's claims record is who **chose to claim** — not a census of every
strike.
