# Week 10

## Communicating risk to visitors and new drivers

### Technically Accurate Is Not The Same As Actually Read

---

## A caution before we start

This unit's dataset doesn't test who's more likely to hit a kangaroo. We
won't claim visitors or new drivers are less careful — no source for that
here.

What we *can* say: a driver with no local reference points needs a
different brief. Regardless of who's holding the wheel.

---

## A fictional notice that fails

> *"...wildlife hazards including but not limited to macropodidae may be
> encountered at any time... refer to clause 14(b)... Section 9 of the
> accompanying documentation pack..."*

Read it at rental-counter speed. What's the one thing you're supposed to
do?

```notes
Give the room 15 seconds with this on screen, then ask. Most won't be able
to answer — that's the point, not a trick.
```

---

## Four stages, most warnings fail at #3

1. **wrote it**
2. **read it**
3. **understood it**
4. **acted on it**

The fictional notice clears 1 and 2. It fails 3 — which makes 4 impossible.

---

## A road sign has the same problem

**From Dr Fenn:** a diamond warning sign clears "wrote it" and "read it"
easily. It says nothing about *when* or *what to do* — so it can't clear
"understood it" for someone with no local calibration either.

Same failure, different medium.

---

## The plain-English hierarchy

> Kangaroos and wallabies cross rural roads, especially at dawn, dusk and
> night. Slow down on rural roads during those hours. If you see one,
> brake steadily — don't swerve. If you hit one, pull over safely and call
> [the after-hours number] — it's on your key tag.

One sentence. Three actions. One pointer.

---

## Rules for expressing risk

- no invented numbers
- "conditions increase risk" ≠ "you will definitely hit one"
- no false guarantees either — "you'll be fine" is equally unsupported

---

## Three reader personas

**First rural drive** — no mental reference at all.

**Different road norms** — nothing to unlearn, no local equivalent existed.

**Jet-lagged and rushed** — needs the fastest possible extraction of what
matters.

Design personas, not a capability judgement.

## Does comprehension prove anything about outcomes?

Bagagiolo et al. (2019) tested migrant farmworkers reading agricultural
safety pictorials in Italy — not road signs, not Australia.

68.8% / 35.4% / 32.4% comprehension across three groups. **No** pictorial
hit the 85% ISO/ANSI threshold.

A structurally similar problem. Not evidence about kangaroo signage.
