## The denominator problem

- raw count needs no denominator — which is exactly the problem
- **per kilometre**: divide by road length
- **per million vehicle-kilometres**: divide by traffic as well

The map has excellent visual authority and no opinion about your
denominator.

---

## Worked exposure calculation

> collisions / (traffic × days × length km) × 1,000,000

| Quantity | Coorabin Rd (busy) | Old Bindari Rd (quiet) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collisions | 18 | 11 |
| Traffic (ADT) | 2,800 | 220 |
| Length | 38 km | 9 km |
| Days observed | 1,409 | 1,409 |
| **Rate / million veh-km** | **0.12** | **3.94** |

Fewer collisions, thirty times the rate. Same formula, same period, opposite
conclusion.

## Reporting coverage

| Coverage | Roads | What limits reporting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Coorabin Rd, Merricourt Hwy | Patrols, cameras |
| Medium | Bunjalung, Grellan Ck, Tindarra | Irregular patrol, carer network |
| Low | Old Bindari Rd, Wombaroo Pass | No routine patrol, patchy phone coverage |

Old Bindari Road has both the highest rate **and** the lowest coverage.
Exposure adjustment does not eliminate reporting bias — it corrects the
denominator, not who was watching.

---

## Choosing a hotspot definition

> A road is a **hotspot** if its rate is at least double the median rate
> across the roads being compared.

- median rate: 1.06 — threshold: 2.13
- one road clears it: **Old Bindari Road**
- a different threshold would draw the line somewhere else — that's why you
  state it

---

## The peer challenge

Swap your ranking and definition with another pair. Ask of theirs:

- does a road get excluded that traffic and reporting coverage suggest it
  shouldn't be?
- is a road included mainly because it's *well reported*, not because it's
  *dangerous*?

---

## Revised conclusion — and next week

> The raw ranking and the exposure-adjusted ranking name different roads as
> the top concern. Both are defensible measures of different things; only
> one of them accounts for how much a road is actually used.

A road with fewer collisions can still be the more concerning road. Next
week asks a different question again: not which road, but what a collision
actually does once it happens — the physics of speed and impact.
