A defensible intervention proposal draws on every strand of the unit at
once: an ecological account of why the strikes happen where they do, a
physical account of what an intervention actually changes about outcome, a
human-factors account of who it needs to work for, and a policy account of
who has to fund and approve it. This closing lecture is about how those
strands combine into one proposal, and how the proposal is marked.

## Outline

- recombining the unit's five strands into a single argument
- what makes a proposal defensible rather than merely plausible
- common failure modes: over-scoping, under-costing, ignoring jurisdiction
- final project: presenting the intervention
