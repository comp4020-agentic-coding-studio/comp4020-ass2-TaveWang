## Before the session

Read the three scene cards below, and the [contact-resource
table](/lectures/week-09/#a-real-contact-resource-table) in this week's
lecture.

## The three scene cards

**Scene A.** The driver has pulled over safely, hazard lights on. When asked
what they saw, they say: "there was a kangaroo, hurt, near some trees on the
highway somewhere." No road name, no direction, no distance.

**Scene B.** The animal is still near the road edge, clearly distressed.
The driver is considering walking closer to get a photo to help identify the
species before calling anyone.

**Scene C.** The animal has moved out of view into roadside bush. Debris
from the vehicle is still on the road, and visibility for other traffic
approaching the bend is poor.

## In the session — "The Roadside Response Tabletop"

This is explicitly a desk exercise: no real roadside activity, no animal
contact of any kind, at any point.

| Step | Time | Activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 min | Ground rules: this is a tabletop exercise, not a field drill. |
| 2 | 10 min | Read Scene A. Decide: what's safe to do, who to call, and what information to give. |
| 3 | 10 min | Read Scene B. Same three decisions — pay attention to what changes. |
| 4 | 10 min | Read Scene C. Same three decisions again. |
| 5 | 10 min | Compare answers across groups; debrief on what changed between scenes and why. |
| 6 | 5 min | Produce the **No-Second-Rescue Checklist** (see below). |

### No-Second-Rescue Checklist (formative)

A short checklist, in your own words, of what to confirm before doing
anything else at a strike scene, and what never to attempt without training.
This is a formative output for this week's activity. It is genuinely useful
background if you're proposing a driver-facing intervention in the [final
project](/assessments/final-project/), but it is **not** a required
reference there — the final project's own requirements don't list it.

## Afterwards

Keep the checklist as optional background reading. If your final-project
intervention touches driver behaviour immediately after a strike, this
week's material is a reasonable thing to cite — but citing it is your
choice, not a requirement.
