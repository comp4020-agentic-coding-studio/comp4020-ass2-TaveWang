## Before the session

Read the three site cards below and the measure-comparison and cost tables
from this week's lecture. If you kept a note of the technology gap you
identified in Week 6, bring it — it's useful context, not required material.

## The three sites

**Site A — Bunjalung Ridge Road.** 15 km, Central Tablelands, 400 vehicles a
day. Medium reporting coverage from an irregular ranger patrol and an active
local wildlife-carer network. Authority: a small local council with a tightly
constrained annual roads budget. Roadside scrub runs continuously both sides
for most of the road's length — there's no single obvious pinch point.

**Site B — Coorabin Road.** 38 km, Northern Ranges, 2,800 vehicles a day.
High reporting coverage: daily ranger patrols and fixed traffic cameras near
two intersections. Authority: a regional road authority with a moderate
discretionary budget, competing against many other roads in its network.
Two known clustering points sit near creek crossings; the rest of the road
runs lower frequency.

**Site C — Merricourt Highway.** 51 km, Coastal Corridor, 4,500 vehicles a
day. High reporting coverage: routine highway-patrol coverage and a
permanent traffic counter. Authority: a state transport department with a
larger capital budget but a slower, more politically visible approval
pipeline. Strikes are distributed along a long open corridor with no single
obvious pinch point.

## In the session

| Step | Time | Activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 min | Groups of three or four read all three site cards together. |
| 2 | 10 min | Each group proposes one measure per site, with a one-line justification citing the evidence or cost table. |
| 3 | 10 min | Swap site assignments with another group, who raises one counter-scenario for each of your proposed measures (for example: the council's fencing budget is cut by half; the underpass floods every wet season; the highway authority's approval process will take three years longer than the funding cycle allows). |
| 4 | 10 min | Revise your proposal for each site in light of the counter-scenario you were given. |
| 5 | 10 min | Whole-room share-out: each group reports the one revision that most changed their original answer, and why. |
| 6 | 5 min | Write the one-page **"Fence Is Not the Finish Line"** design note (see below). |

### Design note format (formative, not a final-project attachment)

One page, one site of your choice. State: the measure you recommend, the
justification tied to the evidence or cost table, at least one limitation or
risk your revision surfaced (an end-of-fence problem, a budget ceiling, an
approval-timeline mismatch), and what evidence would change your
recommendation. This is a formative output for this week's activity — it is
not required anywhere in `assignment-1` or the `final-project`.

## Afterwards

Keep a note of any site where no engineering measure looked adequate on its
own — those carry directly into Week 8's law-and-liability material, where
the question shifts from "what should be built" to "who has to pay for it,
or answer for its absence."
