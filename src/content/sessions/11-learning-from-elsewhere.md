## Before the session

Read both case cards (Seiler 2005, moose/Sweden; Bissonette & Rosa 2012,
deer/Utah) and the Grellan Creek Road scenario in this week's lecture.

## In the session — "The International Evidence Customs Office"

| Step | Time | Activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 min | Recap the five transfer questions and the outcome-type ladder as a group. |
| 2 | 10 min | Each group is assigned a specific claim (drawn from the case cards or the Grellan Creek scenario) and must argue one of three verdicts: **transfer as-is**, **adapt and test locally**, or **insufficient evidence to use**. |
| 3 | 10 min | Present your verdict and reasoning to another group. |
| 4 | 10 min | The other group cross-questions you: what evidence, if it existed, would change your verdict? |
| 5 | 10 min | Whole-room debrief. Different groups reaching different verdicts on similar claims is an expected, acceptable outcome — there is no forced consensus and no default "cannot transfer" answer to fall back on. |
| 6 | 5 min | Submit the **Evidence Passport** (see below). |

### Evidence Passport (formative)

For your assigned claim: the verdict your group reached, the reasoning
(referencing the five questions), and the specific piece of evidence that
would upgrade or downgrade that verdict. This is a formative output for
this week's activity, not a required final-project attachment — though the
reasoning pattern is directly reusable if your final project draws on
overseas evidence.

## Afterwards

If your final-project intervention leans on a Seiler-, Bissonette-and-Rosa-,
or other overseas-sourced finding, the same five questions apply there —
be explicit in your proposal about which parts of the evidence you're
treating as a transferable mechanism and which parts you're treating as a
locally-untested parameter.
