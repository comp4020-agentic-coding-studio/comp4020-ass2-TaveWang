## Before the session

Read the [fictional bad
notice](/lectures/week-10/#a-fictional-example-of-a-warning-that-fails) in
this week's lecture, and the three reader persona cards below. Draft a
one-page risk briefing aimed at a first-time visitor picking up a rental
car — bring it to the session, even in rough form.

## Three reader personas

These are design personas — a way of checking whether a piece of writing
works for different readers, not a judgement about anyone's actual driving
ability.

**Persona 1 — First rural drive.** Grew up in a large city and has never
driven outside one. No mental reference for what "rural road at dusk" means.

**Persona 2 — Different road norms.** An experienced, confident driver whose
home country's roads simply don't have this hazard — there's nothing to
unlearn, because there was never a local equivalent to begin with.

**Persona 3 — Jet-lagged and rushed.** A competent driver reading a dense
notice at an airport counter at 6 a.m. after a long flight, wanting the
fastest possible extraction of the two or three things that actually
matter.

## In the session — "The Rental Desk Cold-Read Test"

| Step | Time | Activity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 min | Recap the fictional bad notice and the three personas as a group. |
| 2 | 10 min | Finish or refine your draft one-page briefing individually. |
| 3 | 10 min | Swap with a partner for a genuine cold read: they read it unaided, aloud if possible, and flag anything confusing, alarmist, or missing, in real time. |
| 4 | 10 min | Revise your briefing on the spot based on what was flagged. |
| 5 | 10 min | Check your revised briefing against all three personas — would each of them actually get what they need, fast enough? |
| 6 | 5 min | Submit the **Warning That Was Actually Read** (see below). |

### Warning That Was Actually Read (formative)

Your revised one-page briefing, plus a two-line note on what the cold read
caught that you hadn't noticed writing it. This is a formative output for
this week's activity, not a required final-project attachment — though if
your final-project intervention is a communication artefact, using this as
inspiration is entirely reasonable.

## Afterwards

Keep the revised briefing if you found the exercise useful — it's a
candidate starting point if your final-project intervention involves
driver-facing communication, but it is optional, not a required attachment.
