Weeks 1 through 6 gave you the ecology of macropod road behaviour, the
strike dataset, spatial and temporal clustering, impact mechanics, and human
perception at dusk. This assignment asks you to turn that into two connected
pieces of research work: a critical review of the published literature on
wildlife-vehicle collisions, and your own hotspot analysis of the regional
dataset used across those sessions.

## The brief

> Review the literature on wildlife-vehicle collision hotspots critically
> enough to identify where it disagrees with itself, then produce your own
> hotspot analysis of the provided dataset and say where it agrees or
> disagrees with what you found.

A strong review doesn't just report what the literature says — it notices
when two well-cited sources define "hotspot" differently, or when a strong
result doesn't survive an exposure adjustment, and says so. A strong hotspot
analysis states its method plainly enough that a reader could reproduce it,
and is honest about where the underlying reporting bias (week 3) limits what
it can claim.

## What you submit

A single document combining the literature review and your hotspot analysis,
with the analysis itself (code, notebook, or spreadsheet, your choice of
tool) attached so the method is checkable. State your sources in full and
declare any assistance used in producing either half.

synthesis of the literature and rigour of the hotspot analysis are each worth
half the mark.
